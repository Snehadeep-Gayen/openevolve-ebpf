1. **Behavior & performance (precise)**  
- Low rates stayed healthy: the new selector always derives a single slot from `reuse->hash` and calls `bpf_sk_select_reuseport` once, cutting instructions (`results/experiments/exp-20251113-162706/agent.c:4-84`). Combined with the server’s fixed 10 µs/2 ms busy-wait handlers (`internal/server/server.go:286-321`), p99 stayed near 5 ms at 50–60 k req/s (`results/experiments/exp-20251113-162706/summary.json:31-63`).  
- Collapse begins at 80 k: overall p99 jumped to 4.89 s with throughput only 66 k req/s (`results/experiments/exp-20251113-162746/workload_summary.json:5-23`), and the client reported 135 k missing replies plus sustained outstanding growth past 188 k (`results/run-20251113-162746/logs/client.log:2-17`).  
- Server logs show the bottleneck is uneven reuseport steering: only workers {0,1,3,5} received traffic initially and, after 10 s, only worker 5 kept receiving ~25 k req/s while others went idle, yet each completed GET still took the configured ≈10 µs (`results/run-20251113-162746/logs/server/server.log:4-66`). That means queueing happens before `handleRequest`, i.e., at socket queues or in userspace scheduling.  
- The same pattern intensifies at 100 k and 140 k: p99 is 4.47–6.51 s (`results/experiments/exp-20251113-162706/summary.json:92-124`), outstanding counts exceed 181 k/361 k with 110 k/361 k lost responses (`results/run-20251113-162806/logs/client.log:2-17`, `results/run-20251113-162826/logs/client.log:2-17`), and server stats shrink from four active workers to one or two (`results/run-20251113-162806/logs/server/server.log:4-66`, `results/run-20251113-162826/logs/server/server.log:4-70`).  
- Root cause: the policy enforces strict per-flow stickiness via the reuseport hash, but the client opens only seven long-lived UDP sockets (`cmd/workload_client/main.go:175-220`). Hash collisions pin those sockets onto a subset of workers, so the effective capacity tops out around the sum of those workers’ single-thread service rates; once offered load exceeds ~60–90 k req/s, UDP queues explode even though other workers sit idle.  
- Compared to the previous program (per supplied parent metrics, **uncertain** because no local file to cite), the new design markedly improves 60 k p99 (≈4.7 ms vs 18.8 ms) but regresses badly beyond 80 k (multi-second tails instead of sub-second).  
- **Confidence: Medium** (strong evidence from logs and code, but no direct visibility into kernel queue depths or parent-run artifacts).

2. **Did the behavior achieve the program’s intent? Why or why not?**  
- Intent: keep per-flow stickiness while lowering hot-path cost by computing one hash-based slot and issuing a single helper call (`results/experiments/exp-20251113-162706/agent.c:4-84`).  
- Success at light load: instruction-count reduction cut mid-rate latency to ~5 ms and met the goal of “simplified, verifier-friendly” selection up to 60 k (`results/experiments/exp-20251113-162706/summary.json:31-63`).  
- Failure at high load: the same “stick to one hashed slot” policy left half the worker sockets unused (server logs cited above), so fairness/throughput goals were not achieved. With only 7 client sockets feeding the hash, collisions pinned most traffic to one worker, producing multi-second p99s and massive drops despite idle peers.  
- Net: the program meets the “single-shot” implementation intent but fails the broader aim of sustaining high-rate throughput/fairness across the worker pool once input load exceeds the capacity of the few workers that end up selected.

3. **Missing signals & new metrics/logs to add**  
- **Per-slot hit counters** — *How*: extend the eBPF program with a parallel `BPF_MAP_TYPE_PERCPU_ARRAY` indexed by slot to `bpf_spin_lock()`/increment on each selection, and dump it in the existing stats ticker. *Why*: would confirm exactly how many reuseport slots receive traffic and quantify collision rates without inferring from indirect worker logs; useful to decide whether to add client sockets or change hashing.  
- **Per-worker queue/backlog depth** — *How*: record `SO_RXQ_OVFL`/`SO_INCOMING_CPU` stats or timestamp packets at receipt to compute time-in-queue, then emit them alongside `[perf] worker=` lines. *Why*: would distinguish UDP socket drops from userspace scheduling issues and show whether multi-second tails stem from kernel queues, user goroutine backlog, or downstream processing.
